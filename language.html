Nihongo 9 – Quarter 3: Hiragana, Katakana, Particles, Vocabulary & Numbers (1–100)

Lesson 1: Hiragana (ひらがな)

1.1 Overview & Usage

  • Hiragana: The foundational Japanese phonetic syllabary made up of 46 basic characters, each representing a specific syllable sound.
  • Primary Uses:
    • Native Japanese words that do not use Kanji.
    • Grammatical elements such as particles (wa, o, ni, de) and verb/adjective endings (okurigana).
    • Furigana (small phonetic guides written above Kanji to show how they are read).

1.3 Hiragana Sound Rules & Modifiers

  • Dakuten (゛) & Handakuten (゜): Small diacritic marks placed on the top-right corner of specific characters to change unvoiced consonants into voiced sounds:
    • K → G: が (ga), ぎ (gi), ぐ (gu), げ (ge), ご (go)
    • S → Z: ざ (za), じ (ji), ず (zu), ぜ (ze), ぞ (zo)
    • T → D: だ (da), ぢ (ji), づ (zu), で (de), ど (do)
    • H → B: ば (ba), び (bi), ぶ (bu), べ (be), ぼ (bo)
    • H → P (Handakuten): ぱ (pa), ぴ (pi), ぷ (pu), ぺ (pe), ぽ (po)
  • Yōon (Contracted Sounds): Combining an -i column character with a small ゃ (ya), ゅ (yu), or ょ (yo) creates a single blended syllable (e.g., き + ゃ = きゃ kya; し + ゅ = しゅ shu; ち + ょ = ちょ cho).
  • Sokuon (Small っ): Doubles the consonant sound that immediately follows it, creating a brief pause (e.g., きって kitte – stamp).

Lesson 2: Katakana (カタカナ)

2.1 Overview & Usage

  • Katakana: The second Japanese phonetic syllabary, featuring sharper, more angular strokes than Hiragana while representing the exact same 46 syllable sounds.
  • Primary Uses:
    • Gairaigo (Loanwords): Words borrowed from foreign languages like English (e.g., コンピューター konpyūtā – computer).
    • Foreign Names & Places: Non-Japanese personal names, countries, and cities (e.g., フィリピン Firipin – Philippines).
    • Onomatopoeia & Emphasis: Sound effects, animal noises, and visual emphasis (similar to italics in English).

2.3 Special Katakana Rules & Commonly Confused Pairs

  • Chōonpu (ー): A long dash used in Katakana to extend the preceding vowel sound by one beat (e.g., ケーキ kēki – cake; ノート nōto – notebook).
  • Look-Alike Characters (Stroke Direction Check):
    • シ (shi) vs. ツ (tsu): シ (shi) has horizontal-leaning ticks and sweeps upward from bottom to top; ツ (tsu) has vertical-leaning ticks and sweeps downward from top to bottom.
    • ン (n) vs. ソ (so): ン (n) sweeps upward like シ; ソ (so) sweeps downward like ツ.

Lesson 3: Japanese Particles (助詞 – Joshi)

3.1 How Japanese Particles Work

  • Definition: Particles (joshi) are short Hiragana words placed immediately after a noun, phrase, or sentence to show its grammatical role (topic, object, location, direction, time, etc.).
  • Sentence Structure: Japanese follows a Subject – Object – Verb (SOV) word order, meaning the verb always goes at the very end.
  • Spelling vs. Pronunciation Rule: Three particles keep their traditional Hiragana spelling but change pronunciation when used as particles:
    • は (normally ha) → pronounced "wa"
    • を (normally wo) → pronounced "o"
    • へ (normally he) → pronounced "e"

3.2 Complete Guide to Discussed Particles (with Multiple Uses & Examples)

Particle & Reading Functions / Uses Examples (Hiragana / Romaji / English)
は
wa
(written as ha)		 1. Topic Marker: Introduces what the sentence is about ("As for...").
2. Contrast Marker: Contrasts two things ("X is..., but Y is..."). 		1. わたしはがくせいです。
Watashi wa gakusei desu. (I am a student.)
2. にくはたべますが、さかなはたべません。
Niku wa tabemasu ga, sakana wa tabemasen. (I eat meat, but I don't eat fish.)
が
ga		 1. Subject / Identifier Marker: Highlights who or what performs an action.
2. Likes, Wants & Existence: Used with suki (like), hoshii (want), arimasu/imasu (exist).
3. Conjunction ("But"): Connects two contrasting clauses at the end of the first clause. 		1. だれがきましたか。
Dare ga kimashita ka. (Who came?)
2. いぬがすきです。
Inu ga suki desu. (I like dogs.)
3. にほんごはむずかしいですが、おもしろいです。
Nihongo wa muzukashii desu ga, omoshiroi desu. (Japanese is hard, but it's interesting.)
を
o (or wo)		 1. Direct Object Marker: Marks the noun directly receiving the action of a verb.
2. Movement Through / Leaving a Space: Used with verbs like walking through, crossing, or leaving. 		1. パンをたべます。
Pan o tabemasu. (I eat bread.)
2. こうえんをさんぽします。
Kōen o sanpo shimasu. (I take a walk through the park.)
3. うちをでます。
Uchi o demasu. (I leave the house.)
に
ni		 1. Specific Time: Marks exact clock time, days, or dates.
2. Destination / Arrival Point: Marks where someone is going or arriving.
3. Static Location (Existence): Marks where someone/something is located (imasu / arimasu). 		1. ろくじにおきます。
Roku-ji ni okimasu. (I wake up at 6 o'clock.)
2. がっこうにいきます。
Gakkō ni ikimasu. (I go to school.)
3. つくえのうえにほんがあります。
Tsukue no ue ni hon ga arimasu. (There is a book on the desk.)
で
de		 1. Location of Action: Marks where an activity takes place.
2. Means / Tool / Transport: Marks "by" or "using" (vehicle, language, tool). 		1. としょかんでべんきょうします。
Toshokan de benkyō shimasu. (I study at the library.)
2. バスでいきます。
Basu de ikimasu. (I go by bus.)
3. はしでたべます。
Hashi de tabemasu. (I eat with/using chopsticks.)
へ
e
(written as he)		 1. Direction Marker: Emphasizes the general direction or heading toward a place ("toward...").
(Note: へ and に are the exact same character—へ is pronounced "e" when used as a particle.) 		1. にほんへいきます。
Nihon e ikimasu. (I am heading to/toward Japan.)
2. うちへかえります。
Uchi e kaerimasu. (I return home.)
の
no		 1. Possession / Ownership: Works like 's or of.
2. Noun Modifier / Description: Connects two nouns where the first describes the second (type, origin, affiliation).
3. Pronoun ("One"): Replaces a repeated noun ("the red one", "mine"). 		1. わたしのかばん
Watashi no kaban (My bag)
2. にほんごのせんせい
Nihongo no sensei (Japanese language teacher)
3. これはわたしのです。
Kore wa watashi no desu. (This is mine.)
と
to		 1. Complete List ("And"): Connects nouns exhaustively (implies a complete list).
2. Companion ("Together with"): Marks the person you do an action with.
3. Quotation Marker: Marks thoughts or spoken words before iimasu (say) or omoimasu (think). 		1. りんごとバナナとみかんをかいました。
Ringo to banana to mikan o kaimashita. (I bought apples, bananas, and oranges.)
2. ともだちとえいがをみます。
Tomodachi to eiga o mimasu. (I watch a movie with a friend.)
3. 「おはよう」といいました。
"Ohayō" to iimashita. (He/She said, "Good morning.")
か
ka		 1. Question Marker: Placed at the end of a sentence to make it a question (replaces ?).
2. Alternative ("Or"): Placed between two nouns to mean "A or B". 		1. がくせいですか。
Gakusei desu ka. (Are you a student?)
2. コーヒーかおちゃをのみますか。
Kōhī ka ocha o nomimasu ka. (Will you drink coffee or tea?)
や
ya		 1. Incomplete List ("And / Such as"): Lists examples of nouns while implying there are other items not mentioned (often paired with など / nado). 1. つくえのうえにペンやノートがあります。
Tsukue no ue ni pen ya nōto ga arimasu. (On the desk, there are pens, notebooks, and so on.)
2. りんごやバナナをたべます。
Ringo ya banana o tabemasu. (I eat things like apples and bananas.)
よ
yo		 1. Sentence-Ending Emphasis ("You know / I tell you"): Placed at the very end of a sentence to share new information, give advice, or add certainty. 1. このケーキはおいしいですよ。
Kono kēki wa oishii desu yo. (This cake is delicious, you know!)
2. あしたはテストですよ。
Ashita wa tesuto desu yo. (Tomorrow is the test, remember!)
も
mo		 1. Inclusive Marker ("Also / Too"): Replaces は, が, or を to show similarity.
2. "Both... and..." / "Neither... nor...": Used twice (A mo B mo) to include both items. 		1. わたしもがくせいです。
Watashi mo gakusei desu. (I am also a student.)
2. いぬもねこもすきです。
Inu mo neko mo suki desu. (I like both dogs and cats.)
から
kara		 1. Starting Point ("From / Since"): Marks where a time period, distance, or action begins.
2. Reason / Cause ("Because / So"): Placed after a clause to explain why. 		1. がっこうははちじからです。
Gakkō wa hachi-ji kara desu. (School starts from 8 o'clock.)
2. フィリピンからきました。
Firipin kara kimashita. (I came from the Philippines.)
3. あついから、まどをあけます。
Atsui kara, mado o akemasu. (Because it's hot, I'll open the window.)
まで
made		 1. Ending Point / Limit ("Until / Up to / To"): Marks where a time period, distance, or action ends (often paired with から).
2. Extreme Emphasis ("Even"): Shows an unexpected extent ("even X"). 		1. はちじからさんじまでべんきょうします。
Hachi-ji kara san-ji made benkyō shimasu. (I study from 8:00 until 3:00.)
2. えきまであるきます。
Eki made arukimasu. (I walk to/as far as the station.)
3. こどもまでしっています。
Kodomo made shitte imasu. (Even children know it.)
など
nado		 1. "Et cetera / And so on / Things like": Placed at the end of a list of examples (frequently paired with や / ya). 1. かばんのなかにほんやペンなどがあります。
Kaban no naka ni hon ya pen nado ga arimasu. (Inside the bag there are books, pens, etc.)
2. アニメやゲームなどがすきです。
Anime ya gēmu nado ga suki desu. (I like anime, games, and things like that.)

Sentence Pattern Breakdown

/* Basic Japanese Sentence Formula */
[Topic] は  [Time] に  [Place] で  [Companion] と  [Object] を  [Verb]。

/* Complete Example */
わたし は  しちじ に  うち で  かぞく と  ごはん を  たべます。
Watashi wa shichi-ji ni uchi de kazoku to gohan o tabemasu.
(As for me, at 7 o'clock, at home, with family, rice/a meal, I eat.)
→ "I eat a meal at home with my family at 7 o'clock."

Lesson 5: Japanese Numbers 1 to 100 (数字 – Sūji)

5.1 Number Building Rules (1–100)

  • 1–10: Memorize the base digits first.
  • 11–19: Say じゅう (jū = 10) followed by the ones digit (e.g., 14 = じゅうよん jū-yon).
  • Tens (20, 30, ..., 90): Say the tens digit followed by じゅう (jū) (e.g., 20 = にじゅう ni-jū; 50 = ごじゅう go-jū).
  • Two-Digit Numbers (21–99): Combine [Tens] + [Ones] (e.g., 37 = さんじゅうなな san-jū-nana).
  • 100: Written as ひゃく and read as hyaku.
  • Special Readings to Watch For:
    • 4: yon (standard in counting) or shi.
    • 7: nana (standard in counting) or shichi.
    • 9: kyū (standard in counting) or ku.
