MAPEH 9 – Quarter 3: Renaissance & Romantic Arts, NCD Prevention & Sua-ka-Sua

Part 1: Music and Arts

1.1 Historical Timelines: Renaissance Art & Romantic Virtuosos

Understanding how artistic and musical styles evolved helps connect each master's innovations to their historical era:

A. Early to High Renaissance Art Timeline (c. 1386–1564)

Year / Period Artist & Milestone Historical & Artistic Significance
c. 1386 – 1466 Donatello (Early Renaissance Pioneer) Revived classical sculptural realism, linear perspective in low relief (rilievo schiacciato), and freestanding figures in Florence.
c. 1440s Donatello's Bronze David First unsupported freestanding nude bronze statue since classical antiquity, reintroducing the classical contrapposto stance.
1452 – 1519 Leonardo Da Vinci (High Renaissance Polymath) Merged empirical science, human anatomy, optics, and painting into the quintessential "Renaissance Man" ideal.
1475 – 1564 Michelangelo Buonarroti Elevated sculpture, fresco painting, and architecture through monumental anatomy and intense psychological drama.
1483 – 1520 Raphael (Raffaello Sanzio) Synthesized Leonardo's grace and Michelangelo's sculptural power into harmonious, balanced High Renaissance compositions.
1495 – 1498 Leonardo's The Last Supper Masterclass in one-point linear perspective and dramatic psychological reaction painted in Milan.
1498 – 1499 Michelangelo's Pietà Carved from a single block of Carrara marble; balances profound grief with classical serenity.
1501 – 1504 Michelangelo's Marble David Depicts David in tense mental focus just before facing Goliath rather than after victory.
c. 1503 – 1519 Leonardo's Mona Lisa Exemplifies sfumato (smoky tonal transitions) and atmospheric perspective in portraiture.
1508 – 1512 Michelangelo's Sistine Chapel Ceiling Monumental Vatican fresco cycle featuring The Creation of Adam, prophets, and sibyls.
1509 – 1511 Raphael's The School of Athens Vatican fresco uniting ancient Greek philosophers (led by Plato and Aristotle) inside grand classical architecture.

B. Romantic Era & Piano Virtuoso Timeline (c. 1782–1886)

Year / Period Composer / Virtuoso Milestone Musical & Historical Significance
1782 – 1840 Niccolò Paganini (Violin Virtuoso) Set the template for Romantic stage magnetism and extreme technical brilliance (24 Caprices), directly inspiring piano virtuosos.
1810 – 1849 Frédéric Chopin ("Poet of the Piano") Dedicated almost his entire output to solo piano, blending lyrical bel canto melody, rubato, and Polish nationalism.
1811 – 1886 Franz Liszt (Concert Virtuoso Innovator) Revolutionized piano technique, invented the modern solo piano recital, and performed entire programs from memory.
1820s – 1840s Evolution of the Iron-Framed Pianoforte Stronger cast-iron frames, double-escapement action, and expanded octaves enabled louder projection and rapid finger repetition.
1831 Liszt Hears Paganini in Paris Inspired Liszt to practice intensively and translate Paganini's violin fireworks onto the keyboard (later yielding La Campanella).
1830s – 1840s Peak of "Lisztomania" & Chopin's Paris Salons Contrasted two ideals of virtuosity: Liszt's electrifying public concert halls vs. Chopin's intimate, poetic salon performances.
1851 Liszt's Transcendental Études & Grandes études de Paganini Published definitive versions of landmark works pushing technical endurance, octaves, leaps, and expressive depth.

1.2 Michelangelo and Leonardo Da Vinci

  • High Renaissance Shift: Represents the summit of Renaissance technical mastery, anatomical accuracy, spatial harmony, and psychological depth following Early Renaissance experimentation.
  • Leonardo Da Vinci (1452–1519):
    • Scientific & Observational Method: Conducted human dissections and studied optics, water currents, and botany to ground painting in empirical nature.
    • Signature Techniques:
      • Sfumato: Gradual, smoky blending of tones and edges without harsh outlines, creating soft transitions in the Mona Lisa.
      • Chiaroscuro: Strong contrast between light and shadow to model three-dimensional volume.
      • Atmospheric (Aerial) Perspective: Softening details and shifting distant backgrounds toward cool blues to create depth.
    • Landmark Works: Mona Lisa, The Last Supper (experimental tempera/oil-on-plaster mural organized around a vanishing point behind Christ), and Vitruvian Man.
  • Michelangelo Buonarroti (1475–1564):
    • Sculptural Conception (Non-finito): Viewed sculpture as freeing a living figure trapped inside the marble block; left several works partially rough (non-finito) to convey struggle between matter and spirit.
    • Landmark Works:
      • David (Marble): Captures coiled physical tension and intense psychological concentration before confronting Goliath.
      • Pietà (Marble): Combines pyramidal compositional stability, anatomical grace, and quiet sorrow.
      • Sistine Chapel Ceiling (Fresco): Features The Creation of Adam alongside prophets, sibyls, and architectural illusions across the Vatican ceiling.

1.3 Raphael and Donatello

  • Donatello (c. 1386–1466) — Early Renaissance Sculpture:
    • Classical Revival & Autonomy: Restored freestanding sculpture as an independent art form rather than merely architectural decoration.
    • Key Techniques & Works:
      • Bronze David: Freestanding contrapposto stance (weight shifted onto one leg) and classical proportions, accented with a shepherd's hat and sword.
      • Rilievo Schiacciato ("Flattened Relief"): Ultra-low relief carving using subtle surface depth and linear perspective to create spatial illusion (seen in Saint George Slaying the Dragon).
      • Penitent Magdalene: Carved in wood with polychrome finish, prioritizing raw emotional and spiritual realism over idealized beauty.
  • Raphael (1483–1520) — High Renaissance Harmony:
    • Compositional Clarity: Synthesized Leonardo's atmospheric softness and Michelangelo's sculptural weight into clear, graceful, and balanced arrangements.
    • Landmark Works:
      • The School of Athens: Gathers classical thinkers inside soaring Renaissance arches; Plato (pointing upward toward abstract ideals) and Aristotle (gesturing outward toward empirical observation) anchor the center alongside Socrates, Pythagoras, Euclid, and Ptolemy.
      • Madonna Paintings: Works such as Madonna of the Goldfinch use gentle pyramidal groupings and warm, luminous color palettes.

1.4 The Virtuoso Pianists

  • Romantic Era Context: Nineteenth-century Romanticism celebrated individual emotion, dramatic contrast, folk nationalism, and dazzling technical mastery on the modern iron-framed pianoforte.
  • Role of the Virtuoso: Combined extraordinary speed, octaves, arpeggios, and dynamic control with expressive phrasing, transforming solo concerts into major cultural events.
  • Key Figures & Musical Traits:
    • Frédéric Chopin (1810–1849): Focused almost exclusively on the piano; paired ornate filigree with singing cantabile melodies, rubato (expressive rhythmic flexibility), and Polish dance forms like the mazurka and polonaise (Nocturnes, Preludes, Ballades).
    • Franz Liszt (1811–1886): Pioneered the solo piano recital format and playing from memory; pushed keyboard limits with works like La Campanella (from Grandes études de Paganini) and the Transcendental Études.
    • Niccolò Paganini (1782–1840) — Violin Influence: Revolutionized violin technique (24 Caprices) and charismatic stage performance, inspiring Liszt to achieve comparable brilliance on the piano.

Part 2: PE and Health

2.1 Non-Communicable Diseases (NCDs)

  • Definition: Medical conditions or chronic diseases that are not infectious and cannot be transmitted from person to person. They typically progress slowly and require long-term management.
  • Four Major Types of NCDs:
    • Cardiovascular Diseases (CVDs): Disorders of the heart and blood vessels (such as coronary heart disease, hypertension, heart attack, and stroke), often linked to atherosclerosis (plaque buildup in arteries).
    • Cancers: Uncontrolled growth and spread of abnormal cells capable of invading nearby tissues or metastasizing to other organs.
    • Chronic Respiratory Diseases: Long-term conditions affecting the airways and lungs (such as asthma and chronic obstructive pulmonary disease, or COPD).
    • Diabetes Mellitus: Metabolic disorders marked by chronically elevated blood glucose due to insufficient insulin production (Type 1), insulin resistance (Type 2), or both.
Risk Factor Category Examples Can It Be Changed?
Modifiable Behavioral Risk Factors Tobacco use/secondhand smoke, physical inactivity, unhealthy diet (high sugar, salt, trans/saturated fats), harmful alcohol use Yes — through daily habits and lifestyle choices
Modifiable Metabolic Risk Factors High blood pressure (hypertension), elevated blood glucose, abnormal blood lipids (high LDL cholesterol), overweight/obesity Yes — through lifestyle changes and medical care
Non-Modifiable Risk Factors Age, biological sex, genetics, and family medical history No — requires regular monitoring and early screening

2.2 Measures and Strategies To Prevent Non-Communicable Diseases

  1. Primary Prevention (Stopping Disease Before It Starts):
    • Eating a balanced diet rich in vegetables, fruits, whole grains, and lean proteins while limiting ultra-processed foods, excess sodium, and added sugars.
    • Engaging in regular physical activity and avoiding sedentary routines.
    • Avoiding all tobacco/vape products and limiting or avoiding alcohol.
    • Getting recommended vaccines that prevent cancer-causing infections (such as HPV and Hepatitis B).
  2. Secondary Prevention (Early Detection & Screening):
    • Routine health check-ups to catch silent risk factors early: blood pressure monitoring, fasting blood glucose/HbA1c tests, lipid/cholesterol panels, and age-appropriate cancer screenings (such as Pap smears and mammograms).
  3. Tertiary Prevention (Managing Existing NCDs):
    • Slowing disease progression and preventing complications through medication adherence, cardiac or pulmonary rehabilitation, blood sugar monitoring, and supervised nutrition and exercise plans.
  4. Community & Public Health Strategies:
    • Smoke-free public policies, excise taxes on tobacco and sugar-sweetened beverages, clear nutrition labeling, and active school/community spaces.

2.3 Fitness and Non-Communicable Diseases Prevention

  • Cardiorespiratory & Vascular Health: Regular aerobic exercise (such as brisk walking, running, cycling, or swimming) strengthens the heart muscle, improves blood vessel elasticity, lowers resting blood pressure, raises HDL ("good") cholesterol, and reduces LDL cholesterol and triglycerides.
  • Blood Sugar & Metabolic Regulation: Active muscles absorb glucose more efficiently and improve insulin sensitivity, stabilizing blood sugar levels and lowering Type 2 diabetes risk.
  • Healthy Body Composition & Lower Inflammation: Combining aerobic exercise with strength/resistance training builds lean muscle mass (increasing resting metabolic rate), maintains a healthy body weight, and reduces chronic systemic inflammation linked to metabolic syndrome and several cancers.
  • Musculoskeletal & Mental Resilience: Weight-bearing movement strengthens bones and joints while reducing chronic stress, anxiety, and poor sleep patterns that contribute to NCD risk.

2.4 The Dance: Sua-ka-Sua

  • Origin & People: A traditional courtship and celebratory folk dance of the Tausug people from Jolo, Sulu (Sulu Archipelago, Mindanao, Philippines).
  • Meaning & Cultural Symbolism:
    • Sua-ka-Sua (also spelled Sua-Ku-Sua) translates to "my pomelo tree."
    • Pomelo trees are a vital source of livelihood and pride in Sulu; their swaying branches, fragrant blossoms, and abundant fruit symbolize grace, prosperity, and a fruitful relationship.
  • Props, Movements & Attire:
    • White Fans (Apid-Apid): Dancers hold two white fans and perform fluid wrist rotations, sweeps, and fluttering motions to imitate pomelo leaves and blossoms rustling in the breeze.
    • Footwork & Posture: Features gentle, grounded steps, rhythmic swaying, and poised upper-body movements that mirror branches moving with the wind while expressing warmth and courtship.
    • Traditional Costuming: Worn in traditional Tausug attire, such as fitted blouses (biyatawi / barawasi), loose trousers (sawwal / kantyu), woven sashes or malong accents, and traditional ornaments.
