Values Education & Philippine Legislation Exam Review Notes

1. Natural Laws vs. Man-Made Laws

Natural Laws

  • Definition: Universal, unwritten moral principles inherent to human nature and discoverable through human reason alone, independent of any government or authority.
  • Key Principles: Immutable and eternal (do not change over time or across borders). Focuses on intrinsic human rights, human dignity, and justice.
  • Core Examples: The right to life and self-preservation, personal liberty, and the fundamental duty to do good and avoid evil.

Man-Made Laws (10 Specific Statutory Examples)

Formal rules created, enacted, and enforced by human institutions (governments or legislatures) to maintain order in society (also known as positive law).

  1. PD 1829 – Penalizing Obstruction of Apprehension and Prosecution of Criminal Offenders: Penalizes acts that knowingly obstruct, delay, or frustrate the investigation or prosecution of criminal offenders (e.g., harboring fugitives, destroying evidence).
  2. RA 7277 – Magna Carta for Disabled Persons: Grants rights and privileges to persons with disabilities (PWDs) to ensure their integration into mainstream society through equal opportunity in employment, education, and health services.
  3. RA 9147 – Wildlife Resources Conservation and Protection Act: Provides for the conservation, protection, and sustainability of wildlife resources and their habitats to maintain ecological balance.
  4. RA 9372 – Human Security Act of 2007: Defines and penalizes acts of terrorism in the Philippines and provides legal measures to combat threats to national security.
  5. RA 8371 – The Indigenous Peoples' Rights Act (IPRA) of 1997: Recognizes, protects, and promotes the rights of Indigenous Cultural Communities / Indigenous Peoples (ICCs/IPs), including rights to their ancestral domains and cultural integrity.
  6. Act of Declaration of Independence (June 12, 1898): Historical proclamation establishing the Philippines as a sovereign nation independent from Spanish rule.
  7. RA 8491 – Flag and Heraldic Code of the Philippines: Regulates the proper design, handling, display, and honor accorded to the national flag, national anthem, motto, coat-of-arms, and other heraldic items.
  8. RA 8749 – Philippine Clean Air Act of 1999: A comprehensive air quality management policy designed to preserve and protect ambient air quality against pollution.
  9. RA 7192 – Women in Development and Nation Building Act: Promotes the integration of women as full and equal partners with men in development and nation-building efforts.
  10. 1987 Philippine Constitution, Art. III, Sec. 4 (Freedom of Speech, Press, & Expression): Guarantees that no law shall be passed abridging the freedom of speech, of expression, or of the press, or the right of the people peaceably to assemble and petition the government for redress of grievances.

2. Youth-Related Laws (Characteristics & Description)

Overview & Definition

Youth-related laws are specialized legal frameworks and statutory provisions enacted specifically to safeguard, empower, and govern the development of the younger population.

Core Characteristics of Youth Legislation

  • Protection from Exploitation & Harm: Explicitly structured to shield minors and young people from physical, emotional, and social hazards, including illegal labor, abuse, neglect, and online dangers.
  • Promotion of General Welfare & Basic Rights: Ensures holistic development by guaranteeing access to quality education, proper healthcare, and supportive environments necessary for healthy transition into adulthood.
  • Civic Participation & Empowerment: Institutionalizes official channels for youth participation in local governance, leadership, and community development (e.g., representation in local government units).
  • Rehabilitative & Restorative Orientation: Prioritizes reform, education, and social reintegration over purely punitive measures when dealing with youth in conflict with the law.

3. Volunteerism

Definition & Core Values

The active practice of donating one's time, skills, and effort for public or community benefit without expecting financial or material compensation.

Key Benefits & Significance

  • Social Impact: Fosters community solidarity, addresses resource shortages in underserved areas, and aids in disaster response and emergency relief operations.
  • Personal Development: Enhances civic responsibility, empathy, communication, leadership, and practical teamwork skills.

4. Labor & Human Work

Definition & Economic Role

  • Composite Effort: Labor is the composite of all human efforts to create goods and services. It is the totality of people’s physical and mental energy spent to achieve a goal. (Example: An architect provides the mental vision and design blueprint, while engineers and construction workers contribute the collective physical effort to build the structure.)
  • Key Component of Production: The adult population determines the size of a country's labor force. However, actual employment and willingness to render skills for wages determine economic contribution; unemployed adults do not directly contribute to economic growth.

Benefits of Labor

Benefit Factor Description & Value
1. Livelihood The cornerstone of seeking employment. People work to earn a living in order to fulfill basic survival needs (food, shelter, and clothing).
2. Human Relationships Reflects the principle "No man is an island." Working with others who share similar interests satisfies the human desire for companionship, belonging, and solidarity.
3. Personal Development Carrying out workplace responsibilities encourages social and intellectual growth, continuously expanding capabilities and skills toward full potential.
4. Job Satisfaction Finding purpose and happiness at work leads to self-actualization. Allows individuals to meet material needs, secure safe working conditions, express creativity, and gain social respect.

Positive Effects of Work (Core Textbook Values)

  • Service: Quality products and useful services result from genuine work (service), which is a valuable asset to societal progress and promotes human dignity, especially when helping the needy or marginalized.
  • Security: Stable employment offers long-term assurance, enabling individuals to make realistic plans for the future without constant financial worry.
  • Success: Devotion and focus in work inspire individuals to overcome challenges, set ambitions, and pursue lifelong learning.
  • Happiness: Wisely choosing an occupation brings joy; beyond earning a living, the opportunity to be of service to others provides fulfillment that money cannot buy.
