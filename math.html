Master Math Exam Review Notes: Geometry, Proofs & Triangles

1. Quadrilaterals

Line Relationships & Geometry Basics

Parallelism: Lines m || n cut by transversal t
Perpendicularity: Intersecting at 90° right angles
  • Parallelism (\(\parallel\)): Coplanar lines, segments, or rays that maintain a fixed distance and never intersect.
  • Perpendicularity (\(\perp\)): Lines, segments, or rays that intersect to form right angles (\(90^\circ\)).
  • Coplanar Lines: Lines that exist within the exact same flat 2D plane.
  • Skew Lines: Non-coplanar lines that do not intersect and are not parallel (exist across different 3D planes).
  • Etc. (Angle Pairs): Alternate interior angles, alternate exterior angles, corresponding angles, and consecutive interior angles.

What's a Polygon & Quadrilateral

  • Polygon: A closed plane figure bounded by three or more line segments intersecting only at endpoints. Interior angle sum: \(S = (n - 2) \times 180^\circ\).
  • Quadrilateral: A 4-sided polygon with interior angles summing to \(360^\circ\).

Specific Quadrilateral Classifications

  • Parallelogram: Two pairs of parallel opposite sides.
  • Rectangle: A parallelogram with four right angles (\(90^\circ\)).
  • Rhombus: A parallelogram with four congruent sides.
  • Squares: A regular quadrilateral combining a rectangle and a rhombus.
  • Trapezoid: Exactly one pair of parallel sides.
    • Trapezoid Types: Right trapezoid (contains right angles), Isosceles trapezoid (congruent legs), Scalene trapezoid (no equal sides).
  • Kite: Two distinct pairs of adjacent, congruent sides.

2. Properties of a Parallelogram

Parallelogram ABCD: Congruent opposite sides, opposite angles, and bisecting diagonals at midpoint M
  • Opposite sides: Opposite sides are parallel and congruent (\(AB \parallel CD\), \(AD \parallel BC\) & \(AB \cong CD\), \(AD \cong BC\)).
  • Opposite angles: Opposite angles are congruent (\(\angle A \cong \angle C\), \(\angle B \cong \angle D\)).
  • Consecutive angles: Consecutive interior angles are supplementary ($180^\circ$).
  • Adjacent angles: Adjacent angles sharing a side are supplementary.
  • Diagonals: Segments connecting opposite vertices.
  • Bisect: Diagonals bisect each other into two equal segments at their intersection point.

3. Trapezoids and Kites

Trapezoid: Bases, Legs, Altitude, and Median
Kite: Perpendicular diagonals & adjacent congruent sides

Trapezoid

  • Trapezoid Properties: Exactly one pair of parallel bases; consecutive interior angles supplementary.
    • Bases: The parallel sides.
    • Legs: The non-parallel sides.
  • Altitude: Perpendicular distance height between bases.
  • Median: Midsegment connecting leg midpoints (\(\text{Median} = \frac{b_1 + b_2}{2}\)).
  • Midpoint: Point dividing segments into equal parts.

Isosceles Trapezoids

  • Properties of Isosceles Trapezoid: Equal legs, congruent base angle pairs, congruent diagonals.
    • Legs (isosceles): Non-parallel legs are equal in length.

Kites

  • Properties of Kite: Two pairs of adjacent equal sides, perpendicular diagonals, main diagonal bisects vertex angles.

4. Inductive and Deductive Reasoning

  • Inductive Reasoning: Reaching a general rule by observing patterns and specific cases.
    • Conjecture: An unproven statement derived from inductive reasoning.
  • Deductive Reasoning: Reaching logical conclusions using accepted definitions, postulates, and theorems.
  • Postulates: Geometric rules accepted as true without proof.
  • Theorem: A proven mathematical statement.
  • Undefined Terms: Fundamental concepts (Point, Line, Plane) accepted without formal definition.
  • Axioms: Universal mathematical rules accepted as true.

5. Congruent Triangles

Triangle Segments: Altitude & Median from vertex
Isosceles Triangle: Vertex Angle & Base Angles
  • Triangle: 3-sided polygon with interior angles summing to \(180^\circ\).
  • Angle Bisector: Ray/segment dividing an angle into two equal halves.
  • Altitude: Perpendicular height segment from vertex to opposite side.
  • Median: Segment connecting vertex to opposite side's midpoint.
  • Midline: Segment connecting midpoints of two sides.
    • Midline Theorem: Midline is parallel to the third side and half its length.
  • Perpendicular Bisector: Line perpendicular to a side at its midpoint.
    • Perpendicular Bisector Theorem: Points on it are equidistant from endpoints.
  • Isosceles Triangle: Triangle with two congruent sides.
    • Base angles: Angles opposite the congruent legs.
    • Vertex angle: Angle enclosed between congruent legs.
    • Theorems in Isosceles Triangle: Base Angles Theorem and converse.
  • Congruent Triangles: Triangles identical in shape and size.
    • Postulates & Theorems:
      • SSS (Side-Side-Side)
      • SAS (Side-Angle-Side)
      • ASA (Angle-Side-Angle)
      • AAS Theorem (Angle-Angle-Side): Labeled a theorem because it is formally derived from ASA and the Triangle Angle Sum Theorem.
  • Right Triangle: Triangle with a \(90^\circ\) right angle.
    • Hypotenuse: Side opposite the right angle.
    • Legs: Sides forming the right angle.
    • Right Triangle Theorems:
      • LL (Leg-Leg)
      • LA (Leg-Angle)
      • HL (Hypotenuse-Leg)
      • HA (Hypotenuse-Angle)

6. Constructing Segments, Angles, Triangles, and Two-Column Proofs

  • Geometric Construction: Precise drawing using only an unmarked straightedge and compass (altitude, angle bisector, median, perpendicular bisector).
  • Two-Column Proof: Structured format for logical geometric proofs.
    • Statement: Sequential claims made from givens to final conclusion.
    • Reason: Formal justification for each step (Definition, Postulate, Theorem).
Statements Reasons
1. \(AB \cong CD\), \(AD \cong BC\) 1. Given
2. Draw diagonal \(AC\) 2. Line Postulate (Two points determine a line)
3. \(AC \cong AC\) 3. Reflexive Property of Congruence
4. \(\triangle ABC \cong \triangle CDA\) 4. SSS Postulate
