Master Math Exam Review Notes: Geometry, Proofs & Triangles

1. Quadrilaterals

Line Relationships & Geometry Basics

Figure 1.1: Parallelism (m || n) cut by transversal t
Figure 1.2: Perpendicularity intersecting at 90°
  • Parallelism (\(\parallel\)): Coplanar lines, segments, or rays that maintain a constant perpendicular distance and never intersect.
  • Perpendicularity (\(\perp\)): Lines, segments, or rays that intersect at a single point to form four \(90^\circ\) right angles.
  • Coplanar Lines: Lines that lie completely within the same flat two-dimensional plane.
  • Skew Lines: Non-coplanar lines that do not intersect and are not parallel (exist across different 3D dimensions).
  • Etc. (Transversal Angle Pairs): Corresponding angles, alternate interior angles, alternate exterior angles, and consecutive interior angles.

What's a Polygon & Quadrilateral

Figure 1.3: General Quadrilateral ABCD (Sum = 360°)
  • Polygon: A closed plane figure bounded by three or more straight line segments meeting at vertices. Interior angle sum formula: \(S = (n - 2) \times 180^\circ\).
  • Quadrilateral: A 4-sided polygon (\(n = 4\)). The sum of its interior angles is always \((4 - 2) \times 180^\circ = 360^\circ\).
Worked Example: Finding a Missing Angle in a Quadrilateral

A quadrilateral has three interior angles measuring \(80^\circ\), \(115^\circ\), and \(95^\circ\). Find the measure of the fourth angle \(x\).

Solution:

\(80^\circ + 115^\circ + 95^\circ + x = 360^\circ\)

\(290^\circ + x = 360^\circ \implies x = 360^\circ - 290^\circ = 70^\circ\)

Specific Quadrilateral Classifications

  • Parallelogram: A quadrilateral with two pairs of parallel opposite sides.
  • Rectangle: A parallelogram with four right angles (\(90^\circ\)).
  • Rhombus: A parallelogram with four congruent sides.
  • Squares: A regular quadrilateral combining both a rectangle and a rhombus.
  • Trapezoid: A quadrilateral with exactly one pair of parallel sides.
    • Trapezoid Types: Right trapezoid (has right angles), Isosceles trapezoid (congruent non-parallel legs), Scalene trapezoid (no equal sides).
  • Kite: A quadrilateral with two distinct pairs of adjacent, congruent sides.

2. Properties of a Parallelogram

Figure 2.1: Parallelogram with opposite sides equal and diagonals bisecting at M
  • Opposite sides: Opposite sides are parallel and congruent (\(AB \parallel CD\), \(AD \parallel BC\) & \(AB \cong CD\), \(AD \cong BC\)).
  • Opposite angles: Opposite interior angles are congruent (\(\angle A \cong \angle C\), \(\angle B \cong \angle D\)).
  • Consecutive angles: Consecutive interior angles are supplementary (add up to \(180^\circ\)).
  • Adjacent angles: Adjacent angles sharing a common side are supplementary.
  • Diagonals: Segments connecting opposite vertices across the interior.
  • Bisect: The diagonals of a parallelogram bisect each other, meeting at their mutual midpoint.
Worked Example: Parallelogram Diagonal Segments

In parallelogram \(EFGH\), diagonals \(EG\) and \(FH\) intersect at point \(M\). If \(EM = 3x + 2\) and \(MG = 20\text{ cm}\), find \(x\).

Solution: Diagonals of a parallelogram bisect each other, so \(EM = MG\).

\(3x + 2 = 20 \implies 3x = 18 \implies x = 6\).

3. Trapezoids and Kites

Figure 3.1: Trapezoid Bases, Altitude, and Median
Figure 3.2: Kite with Perpendicular Diagonals (d₁ ⊥ d₂)

Trapezoid

  • Trapezoid Properties: Exactly one pair of parallel bases; consecutive interior angles between bases are supplementary.
    • Bases: The two parallel sides.
    • Legs: The two non-parallel sides.
  • Altitude: Perpendicular height distance between the bases.
  • Median: Midsegment connecting leg midpoints.
  • Midpoint: Point dividing segments into equal parts.
Trapezoid Median Formula: \(\text{Median} = \frac{\text{Base}_1 + \text{Base}_2}{2}\)
Worked Example: Trapezoid Median Calculation

Find the length of the median of a trapezoid where Base 1 = \(12\text{ cm}\) and Base 2 = \(20\text{ cm}\).

Solution:

\(\text{Median} = \frac{12 + 20}{2} = \frac{32}{2} = 16\text{ cm}\).

Isosceles Trapezoids

  • Properties of Isosceles Trapezoid: Congruent non-parallel legs, equal base angle pairs, congruent diagonals, and supplementary opposite angles.
    • Legs (isosceles): Non-parallel legs are equal in length.

Kites

  • Properties of Kite: Two distinct pairs of adjacent congruent sides, perpendicular diagonals (\(d_1 \perp d_2\)), main diagonal bisects vertex angles and cross diagonal, one pair of opposite angles is congruent.
Kite Area Formula: \(\text{Area} = \frac{d_1 \times d_2}{2}\)
Worked Example: Kite Area Calculation

Find the area of a kite with diagonal lengths \(d_1 = 10\text{ cm}\) and \(d_2 = 14\text{ cm}\).

Solution:

\(\text{Area} = \frac{10 \times 14}{2} = \frac{140}{2} = 70\text{ cm}^2\).

4. Inductive and Deductive Reasoning

Figure 4.1: Inductive (Top) vs. Deductive (Bottom) Logic
  • Inductive Reasoning: Reaching a general conclusion by observing specific patterns, examples, and trends.
    • Conjecture: An unproven working statement arrived at through inductive reasoning.
  • Deductive Reasoning: Reaching a logically sound and certain conclusion using accepted definitions, postulates, axioms, and theorems.
  • Postulates: Geometric statements accepted as true without formal proof.
  • Theorem: A formal statement proven true using logic and definitions.
  • Undefined Terms: Fundamental building blocks of geometry: Point, Line, and Plane.
  • Axioms: Universally accepted mathematical rules or equations.
Worked Example: Inductive Reasoning Pattern

Given the numeric sequence: \(5, 10, 15, 20, \dots\)

Conjecture: Each successive term increases by \(5\). The next two terms are \(25\) and \(30\).

5. Congruent Triangles

Figure 5.1: Triangle Altitude from Vertex
Figure 5.2: Isosceles Triangle Angles
  • Triangle: A 3-sided polygon with interior angles summing to \(180^\circ\).
  • Angle Bisector: Ray/segment dividing an angle into two equal parts.
  • Altitude: Perpendicular height segment from vertex to opposite side.
  • Median: Segment connecting vertex to opposite side's midpoint.
  • Midline: Segment connecting midpoints of two sides of a triangle.
    • Midline Theorem: The midline is parallel to the third side and half its length.
  • Perpendicular Bisector: Line perpendicular to a side at its midpoint.
    • Perpendicular Bisector Theorem: Points on it are equidistant from segment endpoints.
  • Isosceles Triangle: Triangle with two congruent sides.
    • Base angles: Angles opposite the congruent legs.
    • Vertex angle: Angle enclosed between congruent legs.
    • Theorems in Isosceles Triangle: Base Angles Theorem and its converse.
Worked Example: Isosceles Triangle Angle Problem

In an isosceles triangle, the vertex angle measures \(50^\circ\). Find the measure of each base angle.

Solution: Total angles sum to \(180^\circ\). Base angles are equal (\(x\)).

\(50^\circ + x + x = 180^\circ \implies 2x = 130^\circ \implies x = 65^\circ\).

Each base angle is \(65^\circ\).

Congruent Triangles Postulates & Right Triangle Theorems

Figure 5.3: Congruent Triangles Correspondence
  • Congruent Triangles Postulates: SSS (Side-Side-Side), SAS (Side-Angle-Side), ASA (Angle-Side-Angle), AAS Theorem (Angle-Angle-Side).
  • Right Triangle Theorems: LL (Leg-Leg), LA (Leg-Angle), HL (Hypotenuse-Leg), HA (Hypotenuse-Angle).
    • Hypotenuse: The longest side opposite the \(90^\circ\) angle.
    • Legs: The two sides forming the right angle.

6. Constructing Segments, Angles, Triangles, and Two-Column Proofs

  • Geometric Construction: Precise drawing of figures using only an unmarked straightedge and a compass (altitude, angle bisector, median, perpendicular bisector).
  • Two-Column Proof: Structured format presenting logical arguments in geometry.
    • Statement: Mathematical claims made step-by-step from start to finish.
    • Reason: Mathematical justifications using Givens, Definitions, Postulates, or Theorems.
Sample Two-Column Proof: Proving Triangles Congruent

Given: \(AB \parallel CD\), \(AB \cong CD\)

Prove: \(\triangle ABC \cong \triangle CDA\)

Statements (Left Column) Reasons (Right Column)
1. \(AB \parallel CD\), \(AB \cong CD\) 1. Given
2. \(\angle BAC \cong \angle DCA\) 2. Alternate Interior Angles Theorem
3. \(AC \cong AC\) 3. Reflexive Property of Congruence
4. \(\triangle ABC \cong \triangle CDA\) 4. SAS Postulate (Side-Angle-Side)
