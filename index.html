AP Economics Exam Review Notes

1. Demand & Supply

Demand

  • Definition: The willingness and ability of a consumer to buy a specific good or service at a given price within a specific time period.
  • Law of Demand: States that ceteris paribus (all other things being equal), as the price of a product increases, the quantity demanded decreases; as the price decreases, the quantity demanded increases. There is an inverse relationship between price and quantity demanded.
  • Ways to Show Demand:
    • Demand Function: An algebraic equation showing the mathematical relationship between quantity demanded (Qd) and price (P). Example: Qd = a - bP
    • Demand Schedule: A table showing the exact quantity demanded at various price points.
    • Demand Curve: A graph showing a downward-sloping curve (from left to right) that reflects the inverse relationship between price and quantity.

Supply

  • Definition: The willingness and ability of a producer or seller to offer goods and services for sale at various prices.
  • Law of Supply: States that ceteris paribus, as the price of a product increases, the quantity supplied increases; as the price decreases, the quantity supplied decreases. There is a direct/positive relationship between price and quantity supplied.
  • Ways to Show Supply:
    • Supply Function: An algebraic equation showing the mathematical relationship between quantity supplied (Qs) and price (P). Example: Qs = c + dP
    • Supply Schedule: A table showing the quantity supplied by producers at different price levels.
    • Supply Curve: A graph showing an upward-sloping curve (from left to right) that reflects the direct relationship between price and quantity.

2. Market Structures

Market structures describe how industries are organized based on the degree of competition among sellers.

Market Structure Number of Sellers Type of Product Control Over Price Barriers to Entry Real-World Example
Perfect Competition (Perpektong Kompetisyon) Many (Marami) Homogeneous / Identical (Magkakauri) None (Price taker) Very low / None (Napakadali) Agricultural products (e.g., rice, vegetables)
Monopoly (Monopolyo) Single seller (Isa lang) Unique / No close substitute (Walang kapalit) Complete (Price maker) Very high (Napakataas) MERALCO, Manila Water

3. Types of Businesses

Business organizations differ by ownership structure, legal status, and source of capital:

Sole Proprietorship (Isahang Pagmamay-ari)

  • Owner: Single individual (Owner / Owner-Manager)
  • Characteristics: Easy to establish with low initial capital. The owner retains 100% of profits but carries unlimited liability (walang limitasyong pananagutan), meaning personal assets can be seized to clear business debts.
  • Examples: Sari-sari stores, local bakeries, small parlors.

Partnership (Sosyohan)

  • Owners: Two or more individuals (Partners)
  • Characteristics: Capital and skills are combined using a formal agreement (Kasunduan). Profits and liabilities are shared among partners. General partners typically face unlimited liability.
  • Examples: Law firms, accounting firms, medical clinics.

Corporation (Korporasyon)

  • Owners: Stockholders or Shareholders (May-ari ng Sapi), represented by Incorporators (Tagapagtatag) and a Board of Directors.
  • Characteristics: Recognized as a separate legal entity ("artificial person"). Capital is collected through the sale of shares of stock. Owners enjoy limited liability (limitadong pananagutan); risk is restricted to the amount invested in shares.
  • Examples: San Miguel Corporation, SM Investments, Jollibee Foods Corporation.

Cooperative / Cooperation (Koperatiba)

  • Owners: Members (Kasapi) who combine resources to fulfill shared socio-economic goals.
  • Characteristics: Governed democratically under the principle of "One member, one vote" (Isang kasapi, isang boto). Earnings are redistributed to members through dividends or patronage refunds.
  • Examples: Credit cooperatives, transport cooperatives, farmer cooperatives.

4. Consumer Rights & Responsibilities

Under the Consumer Act of the Philippines (RA 7394), consumers are granted rights and corresponding duties:

8 Basic Consumer Rights (Mga Karapatan ng Mamimili)

  1. Right to Basic Needs (Pangunahing Pangangailangan): Access to essential goods and services.
  2. Right to Safety (Kaligtasan): Protection from hazardous products or services.
  3. Right to Information (Impormasyon): Access to accurate product facts.
  4. Right to Choose (Pumili): Access to a variety of goods and services at fair prices.
  5. Right to Representation (Pahayag / Representasyon): Voicing consumer interests in government policies.
  6. Right to Redress (Pagwawasto / Kompensasyon): Repair, replacement, or compensation for defects.
  7. Right to Consumer Education (Edukasyong Pangmamimili): Acquiring knowledge for smart buying decisions.
  8. Right to a Healthy Environment (Malinis na Kapaligiran): Living and working in a pollution-free environment.

Consumer Responsibilities (Mga Tungkulin ng Mamimili)

  • Critical Awareness (Mapanuring Kamalayan): Asking questions regarding quality, pricing, and terms.
  • Action (Pagkilos): Filing legitimate complaints and reporting flawed goods.
  • Social Concern (Panlipunang Pananagutan): Understanding consumption impacts on vulnerable groups.
  • Environmental Awareness (Kamalayan sa Kalikasan): Conserving resources and minimizing waste.
  • Solidarity (Pagkakaisa): Joining forces with other consumer groups.

5. Externalities

An externality (Labas na Epekto) is an unintended side effect or consequence of an economic activity that impacts a third party who did not choose to incur the cost or benefit.

Positive Externalities (Positibong Epekto)

  • Definition: A spillover benefit received by third parties from a transaction.
  • Impact: Markets tend to under-produce or under-consume these goods because social benefits exceed private benefits.
  • Examples: Vaccinations (Bakuna), Education (Edukasyon).
  • Government Policy: Subsidies (Tulong-pampamahalaan), grants, or tax incentives.

Negative Externalities (Negatibong Epekto)

  • Definition: A spillover cost imposed on third parties due to an economic activity.
  • Impact: Markets tend to over-produce these goods because private costs are lower than social costs.
  • Examples: Industrial Pollution (Polusyon), Smoking (Pagsisigarilyo).
  • Government Policy: Pigouvian taxes (Buwis), fines, and environmental safety laws.

6. Market Failure & Role of the Government

Market Failure (Pagkabigo ng Pamilihan)

Occurs when the free market mechanism fails to allocate resources efficiently, resulting in sub-optimal economic outcomes.

  • Abuse of power by Monopolies (Monopolyo).
  • Presence of Externalities.
  • Lack of incentive to produce Public Goods (Pampublikong Produkto) due to the Free-Rider Problem.
  • Asymmetric Information (Di-pantay na Impormasyon).

Role of the Government (Papel ng Pamahalaan)

Intervention Tool Purpose & Mechanics Example
Price Controls (Kuntrol sa Presyo) Establishing legal price boundaries to protect consumers or suppliers. Price Ceiling (Siling sa Presyo) / Price Floor (Lapag sa Presyo)
Taxes & Subsidies (Buwis at Subsidyo) Adjusting market output to correct externalities. Sin taxes / Agricultural subsidies
Regulation & Anti-Trust Laws (Batas sa Kompetisyon) Blocking monopolies and enforcing safety standards. Philippine Competition Act (PCA)
Provision of Public Goods (Pampublikong Serbisyo) Directly supplying infrastructure and essential services. National defense, public roads
