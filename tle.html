TLE Accounting – 3rd Quarter Study Notes

1. The Worksheet

Definition and Purpose

  • What It Is: A common working paper and summary device used by accountants to gather all information needed for preparing financial statements, adjusting entries, closing entries, and the post-closing trial balance on one sheet.
  • Main Purpose: Ensures the arithmetical accuracy of accounting records before adjustments and closing entries are formally journalized in the books.

Steps in Preparing a 10-Column Worksheet

  1. Write the Heading: Centered at the top: Name of Business, Name of Working Paper (Worksheet), and Period Covered.
  2. Unadjusted Trial Balance: Copy account titles and balances from the general ledger; total the Debit and Credit columns to prove equality.
  3. Adjustments: Enter adjusting entries in the Adjustments columns labeled with letters/numbers (e.g., a, b, c). Add new accounts at the bottom if they are not listed in the unadjusted trial balance.
  4. Adjusted Trial Balance: Combine the Unadjusted Trial Balance and Adjustments columns across to the Adjusted Trial Balance columns and verify that total debits equal total credits.
  5. Extend to Financial Statement Columns: Extend Assets, Liabilities, and Owner's Equity to the Balance Sheet columns; extend Revenues and Expenses to the Income Statement columns.
  6. Handle Merchandise Inventory (Direct Extension Method): Extend Beginning Inventory to the Income Statement Debit column. Extend Ending Inventory to the Income Statement Credit column and the Balance Sheet Debit column.
  7. Compute Net Income or Net Loss: Total the last four columns. The difference between the Income Statement columns must equal the difference between the Balance Sheet columns:
    • Net Income: Income Statement Credit > Debit (write the balancing figure under Income Statement Debit and Balance Sheet Credit).
    • Net Loss: Income Statement Debit > Credit (write the balancing figure under Income Statement Credit and Balance Sheet Debit).
  8. Double Rule: Rule double lines under all final column totals.

Rules for Extending Amounts to the Adjusted Trial Balance

Unadjusted Trial Balance Adjustment Column Rule / Action
Debit Debit Add the two amounts (extend to Adjusted Debit)
Debit Credit Subtract Credit from Debit (extend to Adjusted Debit)
Credit Credit Add the two amounts (extend to Adjusted Credit)
Credit Debit Subtract Debit from Credit (extend to Adjusted Credit)

2. Statement of Cost of Goods Sold (Cost of Sales)

Overview

  • Definition: Represents the direct cost of merchandise inventory sold to customers during the period under the periodic inventory system.
  • Role in Financial Statements: Usually a merchandising company's largest expense; deducted from Net Sales to compute Gross Profit (Gross Margin).

Cost of Goods Sold Formula Structure

Component Calculation / Operation
Merchandise Inventory, Beginning Starting inventory balance
Net Purchases Purchases − (Purchase Returns & Allowances + Purchase Discounts)
Net Cost of Purchases Net Purchases + Freight-in
Goods Available for Sale Merchandise Inventory, Beginning + Net Cost of Purchases
Cost of Goods Sold (Cost of Sales) Goods Available for Sale − Merchandise Inventory, End

3. Closing Entries & Post-Closing Trial Balance

Real vs. Nominal Accounts

  • Real (Permanent) Accounts: Balance sheet accounts (Assets, Liabilities, Owner's Capital). Their balances carry forward to the next accounting period and are not closed.
  • Nominal (Temporary) Accounts: Income statement accounts (Revenues, Expenses, Cost accounts) plus Owner's Drawing. They accumulate data for one period only and must be closed to zero.

Four Steps in Closing a Merchandising Business

  1. Close Beginning Inventory & Nominal Accounts with Debit Balances:
    Debit: Income Summary (total)
    Credit: Merchandise Inventory (Beginning), Sales Returns & Allowances, Sales Discounts, Purchases, Freight-in, and all Expense accounts.
  2. Set Up Ending Inventory & Close Nominal Accounts with Credit Balances:
    Debit: Merchandise Inventory (Ending), Sales, Purchase Returns & Allowances, Purchase Discounts, and Other Income accounts.
    Credit: Income Summary (total).
  3. Close Income Summary to Owner's Capital:
    If Net Income: Debit Income Summary; Credit Owner's Capital.
    If Net Loss: Debit Owner's Capital; Credit Income Summary.
  4. Close Owner's Drawing to Owner's Capital:
    Debit: Owner's Capital
    Credit: Owner's Drawing

Post-Closing Trial Balance

  • Purpose: Prepared after posting closing entries to confirm that total debits equal total credits before starting the new accounting period.
  • Accounts Included: Contains only real accounts (Assets, Contra-Assets, Liabilities, and updated Owner's Capital), including the new Ending Merchandise Inventory.

4. Value Added Tax (VAT)

Core Concepts & Accounts

  • Definition: A 12% consumption tax imposed on the purchase and sale of goods and services. VAT is never added directly to the Purchases or Sales accounts.
  • Input Tax (Asset): The 12% VAT paid by the business on purchases of merchandise or supplies.
  • Output Tax (Liability): The 12% VAT collected by the business from customers on sales.

VAT Computation Rules (12% Rate)

  • Exclusive of VAT: Multiply the base price by 12% (0.12) to get the VAT. Add them together for the total invoice amount.
  • Inclusive of VAT: Divide the total invoice amount by 112% (1.12) to get the base price (Sales or Purchases). Subtract the base price from the total (or multiply base by 0.12) to get the VAT.

End-of-Month VAT Settlement

Condition Resulting Account Classification & Action
Output Tax > Input Tax VAT Payable Current Liability — Remitted to the Bureau of Internal Revenue (BIR) the following month.
Input Tax > Output Tax Creditable Input Tax Other Current Asset — Carried over as a tax credit to reduce future VAT payables.

5. Subsidiary Ledgers & Special Journals

Controlling Accounts vs. Subsidiary Ledgers

  • Controlling Account: A general ledger account that shows the total summary balance of a group of related accounts (e.g., Accounts Receivable or Accounts Payable).
  • Subsidiary Ledger: A separate group of individual accounts arranged alphabetically—such as the Accounts Receivable Ledger (customers) and Accounts Payable Ledger (suppliers)—whose balances must equal the controlling account total.

The Four Special Journals & The General Journal

Journal Transactions Recorded Key Examples
Sales Journal (SJ) Sales of merchandise on account (credit sales) only Selling goods to a customer with credit terms (e.g., 2/10, n/30).
Purchases Journal (PJ) Purchases of merchandise on account (credit purchases) only Buying inventory from a supplier on credit.
Cash Receipts Journal (CRJ) All transactions involving an inflow (receipt) of cash Cash sales, customer account collections, cash investments by owner, bank loan proceeds.
Cash Payments Journal (CPJ) All transactions involving an outflow (payment) of cash Cash purchases, paying suppliers, paying utilities/salaries, owner cash withdrawals, loan payments.
General Journal (GJ) Transactions that cannot fit into any of the four special journals Sales/purchase returns on account, non-cash investments/withdrawals, issuing/receiving notes in lieu of accounts, adjusting & closing entries.
Quick Tip for Special Journals: Always check if cash moved first! If cash was received (even a partial down payment on a sale), it goes in the Cash Receipts Journal. If cash was paid out (even a down payment on a purchase), it goes in the Cash Payments Journal.
